## Common (conventional) practice
- Get a soil test; apply N-P-K at the recommended dose for the crop, nitrogen split into 3–4 doses.
- Neem-coated urea, DAP/SSP for phosphorus, MOP for potash; zinc sulphate where zinc is deficient.
- **Strengths:** fast response, predictable yield, little labour, easy to buy.

## Organic / natural practice (Nammalvar's way)
- Farmyard manure / compost / vermicompost before ploughing; green manure (daincha, sunhemp) ploughed in at flowering.
- Liquid inputs with irrigation: jeevamirtham, amirtha karaisal; panchagavya / fish amino acid sprays.
- Mulch, mixed cropping, legumes in rotation; bio-fertilisers (Azospirillum, phosphobacteria).
- **Strengths:** builds soil carbon and water-holding, low cash cost, premium price if certified.

## Side effects & risks of each
| | Chemical fertiliser | Organic inputs |
|---|---|---|
| Soil | Overuse depletes organic carbon; acidification (ammonium fertilisers); salt build-up; micronutrient imbalance; fewer earthworms | Builds soil life; slow to correct severe deficiencies |
| Water | Nitrate leaching into wells; algae in tanks and ponds | Little risk if manure is well composted |
| Pests | Excess nitrogen → soft growth → more leaf folder, BPH, sucking pests | Generally fewer pest outbreaks in diverse, balanced fields |
| Cost | Rises each year; subsidy-dependent; debt risk | Low cash cost, more labour; needs cattle or bought manure |
| Yield | High and quick when soil is still healthy; falls if soil degrades | Can dip 10–30% for 2–3 seasons during transition, then stabilises (varies) |
| Health | Handling risk; no residue concern in grain at correct doses | Fresh/raw manure can carry pathogens — compost it |

## Middle path (integrated nutrient management)
Use the soil-test dose, but supply 25–50% of nitrogen from organic sources, add green manure every year, and stop "extra urea for colour". Convert one plot fully to organic, learn, then expand.
