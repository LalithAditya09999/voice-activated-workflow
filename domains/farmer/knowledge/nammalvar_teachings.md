**Who he was:** Dr. G. Nammalvar (1938–2013), from Thanjavur district, trained in agriculture and worked in government agricultural research before leaving to promote ecological farming. He travelled village to village, teaching through stories and field demonstrations rather than lectures. He campaigned against chemical-intensive farming and GM crops and in defence of the Cauvery delta. He died on 30 December 2013 during a campaign in the delta.

**Core principles (as taught by his movement):**
1. **Feed the soil, not the plant.** Healthy soil life (earthworms, microbes) feeds the crop. Chemical fertiliser feeds the plant and starves the soil.
2. **Never leave soil bare.** Mulch with crop residue, leaves and cover crops; bare soil loses water and life.
3. **Diversity over monoculture.** Mixed and multi-layer cropping (tall trees, medium trees, shrubs, ground crops, climbers/tubers) so the land produces all year and pests don't explode.
4. **Integrate animals.** Native cows, goats, country chickens and fish turn waste into manure and income; dung and urine are the base of farm-made inputs.
5. **Make inputs on the farm.** Panchagavya, amirtha karaisal, fish amino acid, herbal pest repellents — cheap and made from what is at hand (see "Farm-made organic preparations").
6. **Save native seeds** and share them; seed sovereignty keeps farmers free of debt.
7. **Harvest every drop of rain:** farm ponds, bunds, trenches, living fences and trees.
8. **Shift gradually.** Convert part of the land first, learn, then expand — yields can dip in the first seasons while the soil recovers.
