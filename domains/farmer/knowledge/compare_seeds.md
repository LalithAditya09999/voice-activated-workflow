| Type | What it is | Good | Side effects / risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Native / traditional** (e.g. Mappillai Samba, local millets) | Farmer-selected over generations | Save & share seed free; hardy in drought/flood/salinity; low input; premium market; nutrition | Lower yields in many cases; tall varieties can lodge; mixed purity — do a germination test |
| **Improved (open-pollinated / HYV)** (e.g. TNAU "CO", "ADT" varieties) | Bred by research stations, uniform | Higher yield; disease resistance; seed can be saved 2–3 seasons; certified seed subsidised | Often bred for high-input conditions; replace seed every few seasons to keep purity |
| **Hybrid (F1)** (maize, cotton, vegetables, some rice) | Cross of two parent lines | Highest yields and uniformity | Must buy every season (saved seed segregates); costly; usually needs more fertiliser, water and sprays; company dependence |
| **GM** | Gene inserted from another organism | **Bt cotton** — the only GM crop approved for cultivation in India — controls American bollworm | Pink bollworm has become resistant — still needs IPM and refuge rows; seed cost; **illegal herbicide-tolerant (HTBt) seed is sold — buying it is unlawful and unprotected**; GM mustard approval is still under court review — check before believing any seller |

**Honest note on GM safety:** approved GM crops pass safety review; the bigger practical problems for small farmers are cost, resistance in pests, loss of seed-saving and dependence on companies — not the food on the plate. Avoid exaggerated claims either way.

**How to choose:** match seed to your water, season and market. If you want premium/organic markets or low-input farming → native or improved varieties. If you have assured irrigation and buyers for volume → improved or hybrid. Always buy from licensed dealers with a bill and lot number; keep the packet until harvest (needed for complaints and compensation).
